Module HorseCollisionMod.Detection

Detection: whether an impact happened, and which way it pushed.

The footprint test decides whether a nearby NPC is actually under the horse rather than merely close to it, and the direction lookup turns the horse's velocity into the side the victim is struck from, which is what selects the reaction clip.

Attached to the HorseCollisionMod table created by the entry point, which pulls this file in with Script.ReloadScript. The footprint limits are read from Config, so they live in the entry point and exist before this file runs.

Info:

  • Release: 4.6.1
  • Author: jrandall54

HorseCollisionMod:IsInHorseFootprint (npc, horsePos, horseForward, speed)
Tests whether a victim is actually under the horse.

The sphere search is a broad-phase cull and nothing more. A horse is about two meters long and under a meter wide, so a sphere around its origin also catches people walking alongside or trailing behind it, which is what makes collisions feel like they reach too far.

This narrows the sphere to an oriented box: the victim must be within the horse's width laterally, between its rear and front reach along its facing, and at a comparable height. The front reach is extended by the distance the horse travels in one tick so that fast victims are not missed between frames.

Parameters:

  • npc table victim entity
  • horsePos table world position of the horse
  • horseForward table horse facing, unit vector
  • speed number horse speed in meters per second

Returns:

    boolean true when the victim is inside the footprint
HorseCollisionMod:FootprintDetail (npc, horsePos, horseForward, speed)
Measurements for a candidate the footprint test rejected.

  • horsePos
  • horseForward
  • speed

Returns:

    string the distances and the limits they were checked against
HorseCollisionMod:GetImpactDir (npc, velocity, speed)
Works out which side of the victim the impact lands on.

The stagger clips are authored relative to the NPC's facing, so the impact has to be expressed in the victim's own frame. Using the horse's frame instead makes NPCs stagger into the horse rather than away from it.

The impact is derived from the horse's direction of travel, not its position. Position is ambiguous at the moment of contact, when the horse is effectively on top of the victim and a centimeter either way flips the answer. Travel direction is stable, and it is what actually decides which side of the body is struck: a horse moving north hits the south face of whoever is in front of it.

Parameters:

  • npc table victim entity
  • velocity table horse velocity vector
  • speed number horse speed in meters per second

Returns:

    string one of "soforward", "soback", "soleft", "soright"
